03 — System Architecture

The FPGA never touches RF — it just decides.

Digital control and RF signal are on entirely separate paths. An HDL state machine sets the diode bias pattern; a driver and bias network translate that into the DC conditions each diode needs, without loading the RF path.

Why FPGA, specifically

Not talked about much — which is exactly the point.

Most published polarization reconfigurable antenna designs focus almost entirely on the RF structure — the slot geometry, the diode placement, the switching mechanism — while the digital control architecture that actually drives the switching gets little attention. Here, the FPGA generates the digital bias pattern for the PIN diodes through a driver stage and a bias network, letting the polarization state be selected programmatically through an HDL-based state machine rather than fixed, hand-wired control. That makes the switching sequence configurable and scalable to more diodes or more polarization states without redesigning the control hardware.
